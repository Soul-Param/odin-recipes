Momos

Momos are bite-sized, soft flour-dough dumplings.

They were originated from the Himalayan region (traditionally Tibet and Nepal) that have become a beloved staple and street food across India.

Ingredients Required

    For the Momo Wrapper (Dough)

  • All-purpose flour (maida): 1 to 2 cups
  • Salt: ¼ to ½ teaspoon
  • Oil: ½ to 1 teaspoon (neutral oil like sunflower or canola)
  • Water: As needed (lukewarm or normal water for kneading into a firm yet pliable dough)

    • For Vegetable Filling (Veg Momos)

  • Cabbage: 1 to 2 cups (finely chopped or grated)
  • Carrot: ½ to 1 cup (finely chopped or grated)
  • Onion / Spring Onions: ½ to 1 small onion and chopped green spring onions
  • Ginger & Garlic: 1 teaspoon each (finely minced or pasted)
  • Green Chili: 1 (finely chopped, optional)
  • Soy Sauce: ½ to 1 teaspoon
  • Black Pepper Powder: ½ to ¾ teaspoon
  • Oil: 1 to 2 tablespoons (for sautéing the mix)
  • Salt: To taste
  • Optional additions: Paneer, French beans, or butter

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Step 1: Make the Dough

    • Mix ingredients: In a large bowl, combine 2 cups of maida (all-purpose flour), 1/2 teaspoon of salt, and 1 tablespoon of oil.
    • Knead: Slowly add warm water and knead for 4 to 5 minutes until you get a firm yet smooth, semi-soft dough.
    • Rest: Brush a little oil on top, cover it with a damp cloth or bowl, and let it rest for 20 to 30 minutes

  2. Step 2: Prepare the Vegetable Filling.

    • Chop and salt: Finely chop or grate 2 cups of cabbage, 1 small carrot, 1 finely chopped onion, and spring onions. Mix in a pinch of salt and let it sit for 10 minutes.
    • Squeeze water: Squeeze out the excess water from the cabbage and carrots using a clean cloth or your hands so the filling does not turn soggy.
    • Aromatics & seasoning: Heat 1 teaspoon of oil in a pan, sauté 1 teaspoon of finely minced ginger and garlic along with a green chili, then add the squeezed veggies. Stir-fry on high heat for 2 minutes.
    • Flavor: Add 1 teaspoon of soy sauce, 1/2 teaspoon of black pepper powder, and salt to taste. Mix well, remove from the heat, and let it cool completely

  3. Step 3: Roll and Shape the Momos

    • Divide dough: out small, marble-sized balls from the rested dough (about 8–9 grams each).
    • Roll thin: Dust with a little flour and roll each ball into a very thin, small circular disc (about 3 inches wide) keeping the edges slightly thinner than the center.
    • Fill and pleat: Place 1 tablespoon of the vegetable mixture in the center. Lift the edges and make small pleats all the way around, then bring them together in the center and twist to seal it into a potli or round shape

  4. Step 4: Steam the Momos

    • Prep steamer: Grease a steamer basket or plate with oil (or line it with cabbage leaves) so the momos do not stick.
    • Arrange: Place the shaped momos with enough space between them to allow for expansion.
    • Steam: Place the plate inside a steamer or a large pot with boiling water, cover tightly, and steam on medium-high heat for 10 to 12 minutes.
    • Check: They are ready when the outer skin turns shiny, slightly translucent, and no longer feels sticky to the touch.
